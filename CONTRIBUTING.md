# Contributing

Thank you. The more machines we hear about, the more useful this gets for people who can't afford new hardware.

## The most useful thing you can send: your machine's numbers

1. Run [`bench/bench.py`](bench/) (see [bench/README](bench/README.md)), or just note what you got from llama.cpp or Ollama.
2. [Open a "Benchmark result" issue](https://github.com/affordable-home-ai/old-hardware-home-ai/issues/new/choose) and fill in the form: machine, CPU, RAM, model, tokens per second.
3. Failures count too. "It was too slow to use" or "it wouldn't load" saves the next person a weekend.

## Other ways to help

- **Fix or clarify a guide.** If a step confused you, it will confuse others. Open an issue or a pull request.
- **Suggest a free project** that helped you run AI at home (see [Projects we stand on](PROJECTS-WE-STAND-ON.md)).
- **Translate** a guide into another language.

## Keep your privacy (and ours)

Before you post anything, remove:
- IP addresses, MAC addresses and network names,
- serial numbers and service tags (from photos too),
- your name, email, user name and home-folder paths, unless you want them public.

If you send a pull request, run `bash scripts/privacy-check.sh` first.

## Honesty rules

- Say what you **measured** and what you **guessed**. Label estimates as estimates.
- Give the date, the machine and the software version with every number.
- No paid promotion. We only list projects we've used ourselves.

## Be kind

Many people here are new to all of this. Explain things simply, assume good intentions, and never make anyone feel
bad for having old or cheap hardware. That's the whole point.
